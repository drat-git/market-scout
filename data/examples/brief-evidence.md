Which gaps in this research could change the market comparison, and what should an analyst verify first?

## Answer: prioritize gaps that can change the decision

The biggest expansion uncertainty is access and operating feasibility, which the saved provider descriptions do not establish. Within the competitor dataset, prioritize clinic-level service scope and exact locations before drawing conclusions about direct competitors or local coverage.

There are 3 researched records that do not pass every current screening requirement, and 3 documented clinics without a captured street address. These are different problems: a provider can pass the broad eligibility screen while still lacking information needed for a useful business comparison. The priorities below distinguish changes to eligibility, changes to the peer set, and information needed for a business decision.

## Priority 2: complete the geography before site-level comparisons

3 documented clinics lack a captured street address: KidWorks Therapy Services (Austin), Line Leader Pediatric Therapy (Austin), and Brighton Center (San Antonio).

Confirm an operating treatment address rather than assigning a corporate office or an unlinked campus address. Capture the suite, branch name, evidence date, and the therapies available at that site; check map placement against that address. A missing pin must remain visible as a research gap.

Completion means each documented clinic used in a catchment comparison has a verified treatment address and a correctly placed location. Only then define consistent drive-time areas around candidate sites. Address completion improves geographic analysis; it does not by itself add a company or demonstrate more demand.

Check patient eligibility alongside the address. Brighton’s recorded age descriptions differ, so confirm the center-based age range before mapping it as a direct competitor for the proposed patient population.

## Priority 3: measure access, rather than infer it from service lists

For the closest service-model peers, record evaluation lead time and recurring treatment availability separately, by therapy, age group, and payer product. Verify whether multiple disciplines are available to the same child under usable scheduling arrangements. Date every observation and identify its scope, since a general statement about accepting patients is weaker than a specific intake response.

Cross-check the pattern with referral sources before describing an access gap. Record contrary evidence, not just long waits. Completion means a small, comparable set of current observations that can support or reject a specific local access hypothesis. The present dataset supplies none of the capacity or unit-economic evidence needed to settle that hypothesis.

## Priority 4: assess coverage before interpreting an apparent gap

Repeat the same search categories and geographic boundaries in both metros, keeping a log of queries, dates, candidate exclusions, and unresolved cases. Check relevant clinic providers, system-affiliated outpatient services, and alternative delivery settings without assuming they are interchangeable. The reference set includes the three unresolved baseline records; simulated discovery candidates are outside this researched case.

The practical stopping point is sufficient coverage to test the defined catchment question, documented with remaining blind spots. Neither equal provider counts nor a large list alone establishes completeness. A blank service field or sparse map area should trigger targeted research, not an “underserved market” label.

## How the analyst should close the work

Maintain a verification register with one row per issue: affected company and branch, the decision it could change, the exact question, an owner, the source or contact, a date, and an outcome. Prioritize first the issues that could change the comparator set or catchment, then collect the access and operating evidence needed for expansion.

Use explicit outcomes: confirmed, contradicted, or unresolved. Preserve prior evidence when a finding changes. Regenerate the comparison and brief after verified updates, then state which conclusions changed. Changing a display flag or an analyst’s working selection does not verify an underlying claim.

## Research coverage

This memo examines a fixed reference set of 19 unique companies across 20 company–market records, including records with unresolved evidence. Austin contributes 10 companies and 11 documented physical clinics; San Antonio contributes 10 companies and 10 documented physical clinics. A company present in both metros counts in each metro but only once in the unique-company total. The service benchmark is speech, occupational, and physical therapy documented at one clinic.

Screening uses physical clinic presence and at least one core pediatric therapy. The reference set is independent of selections in the demo. Unresolved records contribute supported facts without being treated as verified matches. Sources and findings are fixed for this prepared example. The research is illustrative and does not represent a commissioned expansion mandate.

## Research limits

The provider set was assembled through purposive website research, without an exhaustive search denominator or a representative sampling design. Documented locations are a lower bound on these companies’ networks. Service counts measure supported descriptions, not therapist capacity, appointment availability, clinical quality, revenue, or share of the market. A service not captured in a record is unknown, rather than absent.

Full-metro demographic context cannot identify a clinic’s catchment or translate directly into therapy demand. Independent analyst review remains pending. Current source checking and dated conversations are necessary before using this memo for a business commitment.
