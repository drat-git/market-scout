## Patient eligibility can change which peers matter

Brighton belongs in the service-model shortlist, but its recorded age descriptions alternate between birth–6 and ages 3–6. Confirm which ages receive center-based treatment before using it as a peer for a broader pediatric clinic. The three-therapy match is useful; it does not establish the same addressable patient group.

University Health is a different qualification case. Its saved Robert B. Green branch evidence supports speech and physical therapy without establishing OT there. It remains a relevant competitor for individual therapies, but does not yet qualify for the all-three shortlist. These distinctions show why an analyst should qualify named branches before comparing operating performance.

## Use a common benchmark sheet

For each shortlisted clinic, collect the same dated observations. Start with exact branch, age range, disciplines currently accepting referrals, evaluation and treatment lead times, hours, payer products, and referral requirements. For families needing several therapies, ask whether intake and scheduling are coordinated and whether each discipline is actually available.

Keep observed facts separate from interpretations. Record “not confirmed” when the provider does not disclose a field; do not score it as zero. Published therapist names are not a reliable full-time staffing measure. A hospital-affiliated clinic, an early-childhood program, and a general outpatient clinic should not be assumed to have the same economics merely because each lists the same therapies.

## What would make the shortlist decision-ready

The analyst should return a verified clinic-by-clinic benchmark sheet and a short explanation of which peers remain comparable to the proposed model. Identify at least one concrete operating lesson worth testing—such as intake coordination or a scheduling approach—and the evidence required to assess it.

If a shortlisted clinic’s age range, treatment setting, or access rules differ substantially, retain it in the landscape but explain why it is a weaker operating benchmark. If another provider’s branch roster confirms the missing therapies, add it to the core group. The result should be a reasoned peer set, not a ranking based on the number of services advertised.

## Research coverage

This memo examines a fixed reference set of 19 unique companies across 20 company–market records, including records with unresolved evidence. Austin contributes 10 companies and 11 documented physical clinics; San Antonio contributes 10 companies and 10 documented physical clinics. A company present in both metros counts in each metro but only once in the unique-company total. The service benchmark is speech, occupational, and physical therapy documented at one clinic.

Screening uses physical clinic presence and at least one core pediatric therapy. The reference set is independent of selections in the demo. Unresolved records contribute supported facts without being treated as verified matches. Sources and findings are fixed for this prepared example. The research is illustrative and does not represent a commissioned expansion mandate.

## Research limits

The provider set was assembled through purposive website research, without an exhaustive search denominator or a representative sampling design. Documented locations are a lower bound on these companies’ networks. Service counts measure supported descriptions, not therapist capacity, appointment availability, clinical quality, revenue, or share of the market. A service not captured in a record is unknown, rather than absent.

Full-metro demographic context cannot identify a clinic’s catchment or translate directly into therapy demand. Independent analyst review remains pending. Current source checking and dated conversations are necessary before using this memo for a business commitment.
